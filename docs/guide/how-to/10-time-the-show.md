# How to time the show in rehearsal

Teach the book how long each part of the show really takes, so that on the night the line follows the stage and the countdowns to the cues can be believed.

## Before you start

- A run of the show, or of an act, without stops if possible.
- The cues pinned. They are not needed for timing, but then you see the calls working as you go.
- Decide how fine you want it. **From time to time** is the way a run starts: the line runs and you double-tap a word now and then, once or twice a page. **Line by line** is exact: one press at the end of every speech. Use it for a tricky sequence.

## Steps

1. Press **Rehearse** at the top (or R), then **Timing** (T), the toggle beside Boards, Cues and Ink. The line appears, and the panel on the right with the clock, **Start** and the two ways of timing; the pens and everything else stay at the top. Every speech shows its time at the right edge of the page: `~` in front means it is only inferred from the number of words. The panel takes a third of the window: hide the boards (B) if the page gets small.
2. Scroll to where the run starts.
3. **Double-tap the first word** that will be spoken. The line goes there.
4. As the actor starts, press **Space**. The line runs.
5. **As the show goes on, double-tap the word being spoken.** Each tap says "the show is here": the real time since your last tap is shared among the speeches in between, by their length. The line may have run ahead of the actors or fallen behind them; tap where they are. Arrow down does the same for "the next speech has begun", if your hands are on the keyboard.
   After each one the panel says what was timed: its time now, what it was before, and how many blocks. The panel also counts the time since your last tap.
6. **When the run stops** (a note from the director, a repeat), press **Space** at once. Time with the line stopped is not counted.
7. **To pick up elsewhere:** with the line stopped, double-tap the new place, then Space when they start.
8. At the end, press Space. Press **Timing** again to put the line away and have the whole window for the book, or **Write** (W) for the text.

The times are saved with the show. Timing in Rehearse is where the book learns: in **Show** nothing is learned, whatever you tap. No cue is called in Rehearse: the warnings and standbys belong to Show.

## Write in the book while you time

In Rehearse the line keeps running while you write.

- **A note or a cue:** tap a word once and pick the department (**TODO** for a note to yourself). Type and press Enter.
- **A drawing:** write with the pen. Ctrl+Z takes the last thing back.
- **A board:** press **Boards** (B) to bring the facing page in beside the script.
- **A change to the text:** hold a finger on the speech, or tap a word and press **Edit text**. The line stays in the speech it was in, and a timed speech keeps its time, longer or shorter as you made it. For a longer rewrite, **Write** (W) is the place: the whole script as text.

A double tap is always "the show is here". It never opens the text.

## Line by line, for exact times

1. Press **Line by line** in the panel. The line goes to the start of the speech it is in, and a **Next** button appears.
2. As the actor starts, press **Space**. The line does not run: it waits on the speech while the panel counts.
3. When the speech is over, press **Next** (or Arrow down). The speech gets exactly that time and the line waits on the next one.
4. Go on, one press for each speech. To skip ahead, double-tap a later speech: the ones you skipped share the time.
5. **From time to time** takes you back to the running line.

While the line waits, the calls do not count down. A rough run later leaves these times in proportion: it does not flatten them.

## A stretch with no words

A fight, a dance, a scene change: the script says "(They fight.)" and the stage takes two minutes.

Either mark the word before it and the word after it, with the line running: the action in between gets the time.

Or type it: press the time of that block at the right edge of the page, type `120` or `2:00`, and press Enter. It then shows with `=` in front, and later timings around it leave it alone. An empty box and Enter forget it. **+5 s** and **−5 s** in the panel do the same for the block the line is in.

## If you tapped wrong

Press **Undo** in the panel straight after. It takes back the last timing.

A tap far off (while the line is stopped, backwards, or giving a time that makes no sense) is not kept as a time anyway. It only moves the line, and the panel says why nothing was timed.

## How much is done

The panel shows the share of the show that is timed. You do not need 100%: what is not timed runs at the pace of the show, 130 words a minute to begin with.

Once enough speech is timed, the panel offers the pace it measured ("Rest at … wpm"). Press it and everything not yet timed uses your company's real pace instead of the default. You can also set the number in **Settings** ("Pace of blocks not timed").

## Afterwards

- **Save a revision** ("Timed, run of 20 October"). The times are part of the show and come back with a restore.
- A later rewrite keeps the time of a speech changed on its own, longer or shorter as it became, and forgets the times of speeches rewritten together. Time that scene again at the next run.
- To start over: **Settings** → **Forget … timed blocks**.

## If something is off

- **Nothing is being timed.** **Timing** is not lit in Rehearse, or the clock is not running (Space). Both are needed. The panel says so after a tap that timed nothing.
- **The line ran off while I was not looking.** Stop it, double-tap where the show is, start again. The stretch you missed keeps the times it had.
- **The line is always ahead (or behind).** That is what timing fixes. If it is still so after a timed run, on that night use the pace control, not another timing ([Call a show](11-call-a-show.md)).
