# How to call a show

Run a performance from the book: the line follows the show, the panel tells you what is coming, and nothing on the screen can be changed by accident.

## Before the half

1. **Open the show file** and check the status reads **Saved**.
2. **Check you are in the right variant** (☰ → History…), if the show has more than one.
3. **Save a revision** ("Performance, 24 October"). After tonight you can come back to the book exactly as it was called.
4. **Set the warning times** if the defaults do not suit: **☰** → **Settings…** → "Standby (seconds before the cue)", 30 to begin with, and "Warning", 120. A warning of 0 gives standbys only.
5. Plug the laptop in. Night mode (N) if the desk is dark.

## Start

1. Scroll to the top of the show.
2. Press **Show** (or S). The toolbar and the boards go; the script fills the window; the panel on the right shows the clock and the first calls.
3. Leave **Time it** off. On a show night nothing should be learned.
4. **Double-tap the first word.** The line is there, stopped.
5. On the first word spoken (or on your own go for the opening cue), press **Space**.

## While it runs

**Read the panel.** From the top: how far in, how long in all, what is left and when it ends; then the calls.

| The panel says | It means |
|---|---|
| **WARNING**, with a time | The line reaches that cue in under two minutes |
| **STANDBY**, counting down | Under thirty seconds. Say your standby. |
| **GO** | The line has just passed the cue |
| **Next**, with a time | Nothing close; this is the next cue |

Cues that fall together are one call. On the page, the tags of the cues being called are outlined.

**The call is yours.** The countdown is a clock's estimate of where the actors are. Watch the stage and give the go on the word, as you always have.

**Keep the line honest.** When it drifts:

| The stage is | Do |
|---|---|
| A little ahead or behind | Double-tap the word being spoken |
| One speech on, or back | Arrow down, Arrow up |
| Stopped (a held pause, a show stop) | Space; Space again when they go on |
| Running fast or slow all evening | **+** or **−** beside the percentage (or `]` and `[`) |

After a minute of corrections the panel may offer "Tonight …%: use it". Press it and the line takes tonight's pace.

**Look elsewhere without losing your place.** Scroll, press a call to see its cue, or press the map on the right edge. The line keeps running where it was. **Back to the line** (or Enter) returns.

## Interval

Press Space as the act ends. The clock stops with the line. Double-tap the first word of the next act and press Space when it begins.

## After the show

Press **Leave** twice, or S twice. The toolbar, the boards and your zoom are back.

Nothing in the book has changed. If you saw something to fix (a cue a word late), fix it now, outside show mode, and save a revision.

## The keys

| Key | |
|---|---|
| Space | Start or stop the line |
| ↓ ↑ | Line to the next or previous speech |
| Enter | Back to the line |
| `[` `]` | Pace of the night: slower, faster |
| C, I, N | Cues, ink, night mode |
| L | The cue list |
| + − 0 | Zoom |
| H | Guide to the buttons of show mode |
| S, twice | Leave |

## If something is off

- **I cannot change anything.** Right: that is show mode. Leave it to edit.
- **A single tap does nothing.** By design, so a brush of the hand does not move the line. Double-tap.
- **The line is far from the show after a jump.** Double-tap where they are. It is one gesture however far.
- **The screen dimmed.** The tool asks the browser to keep it awake; not every browser agrees. Set the laptop not to sleep.
- **The countdowns are plainly wrong.** The show has not been timed, or was rewritten since. Tonight, correct the line more often; then [time it](10-time-the-show.md) at the next run.

Until this has been through a few of your own rehearsals, keep a paper book open beside it. See [A clock, not an ear](../explanation/05-a-clock-not-an-ear.md).
