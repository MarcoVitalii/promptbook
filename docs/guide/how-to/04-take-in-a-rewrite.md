# How to take in a rewrite from the author

The author has changed the text. The book already has cues, ink and blocking on the old one. You want the new words in, with everything still attached.

The rule that makes it work: **the change is made in the text that has the cue marks.** The script inside the show is the master copy. A rewrite comes into it; it does not replace it. (Why: [What survives a rewrite](../explanation/02-what-survives-a-rewrite.md).)

## First, always

**Save a revision:** **☰** → **History…**, name it "Before the rewrite of 14 October", **Save revision**. Whatever happens next, this brings everything back.

Then pick the way that fits the size of the change.

## A few lines: type them in

1. Find the speech (Ctrl+F).
2. **Double-tap it.** Its text opens, with any cue marks in braces.
3. Change the words. Leave each `{…}` after the word it belongs to; if that word is cut, move the mark to the word the cue should now be called on.
4. **Ctrl+Enter.**

To add a speech, type it on a new line in the editor of the speech above. To cut one, empty it; or press **Comment out** to hide it from the page and from print without losing it, which suits a cut that may come back.

To move a speech, drag the grip in its left margin to its new place. Its cues and ink go with it.

## A scene or more: the author works on the show's text

Give the author the text that has the marks, and take it back.

1. **☰** → **Edit whole script…**. Select all, copy. Send it to the author as a text file.
2. Tell them one thing: *the bits in braces are cues; leave them after their word, and if you cut the word, leave the braces where they were.* Lines between `%%` are cut text kept aside.
3. When it comes back: **☰** → **Edit whole script…**, select all, paste the new text over the old, **Save**.

The whole change is one step of Undo.

While the text is away, **do not pin or move cues**: they are in your copy and not in the author's, and step 3 would lose them. Ink and boards are safe to work on; they are not in the text.

## The author sends a clean file, with no marks

This is the hard case, and it is better said plainly: a text without the marks has no cues in it. Pasted over the whole script, it removes them all.

Do not paste it whole. Instead:

1. Open the author's file beside the book.
2. Find what changed. If the author cannot tell you, paste their text into a *second* show ("Start from pasted text" in another tab), which gives you their version paged and searchable.
3. Carry the changes across by hand, scene by scene, as in "A few lines" above. For a scene rewritten from top to bottom, paste the new scene into the editor of its first speech and put its cues back from the cue list you printed before starting.

Then ask the author to work from the show's text next time.

## Afterwards, every time

1. **Open Review** (R).
   - **Ink to check**: speeches whose words changed under your handwriting. Tap each **!**: **Ink is fine** or **Clear ink on this block**.
   - **Ink whose text was removed**: notes on speeches that are gone. Delete them, or leave them: if the text returns, so do they.
   - **Ends with no cue to close**, **Lines not recognised**, **Not in the character list**: the usual signs of a mark deleted by mistake, a broken line, or a new character.
2. **Open the cue list** (L) and compare the count per department with the sheet from before. A lower number is a cue that went with its words.
3. **See what changed:** **☰** → **History…** → **Changes** beside the revision you saved first. Read it once through; this is also what you will give the cast ([Tell each actor what changed](07-tell-actors-what-changed.md)).
4. **Save a revision:** "After the rewrite of 14 October".
5. If paper is out: [Reissue only the pages that changed](06-reissue-changed-pages.md). If the cast use the phone script: [send a new file](09-send-to-the-cast.md).

## If it went wrong

- **Cues vanished.** Ctrl+Z straight away brings the old text back. Later: **History…** → **Restore** beside "Before the rewrite". What you have now is kept too, as "Before going to …".
- **Half the script is "not recognised".** The text came back from a word processor with changed quotes, tabs or lower-case names. Undo, and ask for it as plain text.
- **The scene took a long time last week and now the line races through it.** Rewritten speeches lose the time they were given in rehearsal. Time that scene again ([Time the show](10-time-the-show.md)).
